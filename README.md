## 🔬 Cancer and spatial proteomics · KerenLab, Weizmann Institute (2023–2025)

Advisor: Dr. Leeat Keren; collaboration with Dr. Shai Bagon. Spatial proteomics of tissue at single-cell resolution.

Highly multiplexed microscopy images tissue at single-cell resolution, but most analyses use 2D sections of what is really 3D tissue. My work asks how much that sampling choice changes the biology you conclude, and how to recover 3D structure when dense 3D imaging is too costly.

- Showed in simulation and on real multiplexed data that a single 2D section recovers global cell-type abundance reliably, while local statistics (clustering, cell-cell interactions) vary widely, especially for rare cell populations
- Built a geometry-aware reconstruction module that links cells across adjacent sections using phenotype and proximity constraints, then estimates 3D cell centroids with cell-type-specific shape priors
- Quantified the trade-off between section spacing, coverage and redundancy under a fixed imaging budget
- Validated on a public imaging mass cytometry dataset with dense axial sampling, and applied to an in-house CODEX dataset

**Publication:** Harlev I., Oukhanov T., Ben-Uri R., Keren L., Bagon S. *Sampling-Aware 3D Spatial Analysis in Multiplexed Imaging.* CVPR 2026 Workshops (CVMI). [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07890) · [CVF Open Access](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026W/CVMI/html/Harlev_Sampling-Aware_3D_Spatial_Analysis_in_Multiplexed_Imaging_CVPRW_2026_paper.html)

**Award:** Best Poster, *Breaking New Methodologies in Biomedical Research*, Weizmann Institute, July 2025

## ⚙️ Materials science and biomechanics · Soft Materials Lab, Technion (2019–2021)

Mechanics of soft matter and active biological materials, with theory and computation.

- Developed a thermodynamics-based model of how heart muscle cells (cardiomyocytes) remodel under mechanical and hormonal cues
- The model links whole-cell deformation to sarcomere number, sarcomere stretch and myofibril count, with equations for protein production and degradation
- Compared four scenarios (isometric contraction and free radial dilation, each with unlimited or limited protein supply) and found that boundary conditions strongly shape the remodeling response, which may help explain how eccentric and concentric hypertrophy differ

**Publication:** Harlev I., Holmes J.W., Cohen N. *The influence of boundary conditions and protein availability on the remodeling of cardiomyocytes.* Biomechanics and Modeling in Mechanobiology 21(1), 189–201, 2022. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10237-021-01526-5)
